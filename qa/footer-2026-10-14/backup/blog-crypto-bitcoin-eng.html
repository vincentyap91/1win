How Bitcoin Works (Decentralisation, Blockchain, Nodes)

To safely use Bitcoin for betting and casino games, it is important to understand at least the basic principles of how it works. The key elements are decentralisation, blockchain, and network nodes. Below, we will briefly explain each of these terms.

Decentralisation

Decentralisation means there is no single centre of control. Bitcoin has no head office, technical support, bank, or regulator that can shut down the network or reverse a transaction. The rules of operation are embedded in the code, and decisions are made by network participants.

Anyone who runs a special Bitcoin client program becomes part of a distributed system. The network cannot be shut down because it does not depend on a single server or country.

Blockchain

A blockchain is a chain of blocks containing transactions. Each block contains a certain number of user operations. The block is then added to the chain in strict chronological order.

Once recorded in a block, the data cannot be changed without the consent of the majority of network participants. This provides transparency and protection against falsification of transaction history.

For the average user, the blockchain appears as a history of transfers. You can open any block to see the commission amount, confirmation time, and the sender and recipient addresses. For online casinos and bookmakers, this is a convenient way to verify deposit payments.

Nodes

Network nodes are computers that run Bitcoin software. They store a copy of the blockchain and verify transactions and blocks for compliance with protocol rules. Nodes reject incorrect operations, such as attempts to spend the same coins twice.

The more nodes there are, the more stable the network is. For a player, this means that a transfer in BTC does not go through a single vulnerable server. The transaction is verified by dozens and hundreds of independent nodes around the world, ensuring maximum security.

Proof of Work Explained for Beginners

Proof of Work is a mechanism that protects the Bitcoin network from block forgery and spam. It is often referred to as mining. To understand how this process works, it is easier to break it down into stages.

Role of Miners

Miners collect unconfirmed user transactions into a new block. Their task is to prepare this block and find a digital value that confirms the correctness of the work performed. This value must comply with the network rules.

What is a Hash and Why is it Difficult to Find

Each block must have a unique hash. A hash is a set of characters that is obtained after processing the block data with a cryptographic function.

The only way to find a suitable hash is by trial and error. That is why miners use special devices with high computing power. They try millions of options until they find a value that meets the network's requirements.

How a Block is Added to the Chain

The miner who finds the correct hash first transmits the block to the network. The nodes then check it according to the protocol rules. If all conditions are met, the block is added to the blockchain. The miner receives a reward for their work and electricity consumption.

Why Complexity is Needed

The complexity of the Proof-of-Work algorithm is constantly changing.

If there are more miners and blocks are created too quickly, the complexity increases.

If there are fewer participants, the complexity decreases.

This is how the network maintains a stable average block creation time of about ten minutes.

Why Proof of Work is Considered Reliable

It is extremely difficult and expensive to retroactively falsify a blockchain. To do so, an attacker would need to control enormous computing power and spend more resources than honest miners worldwide.

Due to the high cost of an attack, the Bitcoin network is considered stable. Therefore, Proof of Work ensures the security of transactions, including deposits at online casinos and payouts of winnings.

Choosing a Bitcoin Wallet Before Buying

Before purchasing Bitcoin, it is important to choose where you will store your coins. A wallet is a tool that allows you to receive, send, and manage BTC securely. Below are the two main categories of Bitcoin wallets, along with their key differences.

Hot Wallets

Hot wallets are applications connected to the internet. They are convenient for everyday use because transactions can be made quickly. These wallets are available as mobile apps, browser extensions, or desktop programs.

They are ideal for players who make regular deposits at online casinos. However, because they are online, they are more vulnerable to hacking attempts, so users should enable two-factor authentication and set strong passwords.

Cold Wallets

Cold wallets work offline and are considered the safest option for long-term storage. Hardware wallets such as Ledger or Trezor store private keys on a physical device that is not connected to the internet. They protect large amounts of BTC from malware, phishing, and unauthorised access.

Cold wallets are the best choice for players who keep part of their winnings in Bitcoin and want maximum security.

Which Wallet to Choose?

A hot wallet is suitable for everyday deposits, sports betting, and casino transactions. A cold wallet is recommended for storing significant amounts of cryptocurrency.
